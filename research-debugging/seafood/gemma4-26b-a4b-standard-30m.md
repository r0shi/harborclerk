## Sustainable Seafood Choices

### Wild-Caught Seafood
Certain wild-caught species are identified as sustainable options:
* **Wild Shrimp:** South Atlantic wild shrimp and offshore Gulf of Mexico shrimp are considered sustainable by the Monterey Bay Aquarium’s Seafood Watch program [shrimp 3EB, page ?; shrimp 2WY, page ?; Shrimp outline2, page ?]. These populations maintain relatively constant levels due to prolific spawning and a short annual life cycle of 10 to 12 months [shrimp 3EB, page ?; shrimp 2WY, page ?; Shrimp outline2, page ?].
* **Herring:** While wild herring caught via trawlers or purse seines can suffer from "belly burst" (enzymatic softening of the abdominal wall) due to being caught "feedy," herring kept in temporary captivity, such as in weirs, tend to "clean up" (eat less), which is noted to produce higher quality fish [fundy 2PS, page ?; fundy 3, page ?].

## Comparison of Aquaculture Approaches

The corpus describes several distinct methods of aquaculture, ranging from high-density industrial models to low-density naturalistic models.

### Low-Density vs. High-Density Models
* **Low-Density (Veta la Palma model):** In Andalusia, Spain, the Veta la Palma operation utilizes a low-density model in large ponds (approx. 70 hectares each), maintaining only three kilos of fish per cubic meter [AoE-85-text, page ?; veta la palma 3WY, page ?]. This approach reduces stress, prevents disease contagion, and minimizes pollution from fish excrement [AoE-85-text, page ?]. It also utilizes a natural feeding system (shrimp) for sea bass and bream [veta la palma 3WY, page ?].
* **High-Density/Conventional Models:** Industry averages can exceed ten kilos of fish per cubic meter [veta la palma 3WY, page ?]. High crowding in tanks or cages facilitates the rapid spread of disease, often necessitating the use of antibiotics [vetalapalma, page ?; AoE-85July30, page ?].

### Species-Specific Cultivation Methods
* **Mussels (Filter Feeders):** Mussels are fast-growing vegetarians that consume phytoplankton [mussels6 WY, page ?; mussels6, page ?]. They are highly efficient filter feeders and serve as "saltwater canaries in the coal mine" because they quickly reveal pollutants, red tide toxins, and agricultural runoff [mussels9b, page ?; mussels9, page ?; mussels14, page ?; mussels10, page ?; mussels11, page ?; mussels7 WY, page ?; mussels7, page ?; mussels8 WY, page ?]. Cultivation requires thinning spat from several thousand per foot down to approximately 150 per foot [mussels9b, page ?; mussels9, page ?; mussels14, page ?; mussels10, page ?; mussels11, page ?; mussels7 WY, page ?; mussels7, page ?; mussels8 WY, page ?].
* **Shrimp:** Shrimp are well-suited for aquaculture due to their ability to live in high densities and varying salinity levels [shrimp 4, page ?; shrimp 3WY, page ?].
* **Sturgeon/Caviar:** High-quality caviar requires harvesting eggs at the late fourth stage of maturity [caviar 3JC, page ?]. Successful sturgeon farming (*Acipenser transmontanus*) has utilized hydroponic raising and the recycling of water from local aquifers [caviar 5clean, page ?].
* **Trout:** In trout farming, reusing water for cooling can cause off-flavors; one mitigation strategy involves passing cold water from the bottom of a deep dam through the fish once before releasing it downstream [caviar 2, page ?; caviar 3, page ?; caviar 2clean, page ?].

## Environmental and Biological Challenges

While aquaculture was intended to alleviate pressure on wild stocks, it has introduced several ecological risks:

* **Disease and Chemical Use:** Crowding leads to disease spread and antibiotic reliance [vetalapalma, page ?; AoE-85July30, page ?]. Shrimp farms specifically use pesticides to control algae blooms [shrimp 4, page ?; shrimp 3WY, page ?]. Furthermore, the use of industrial chicken pellets in some operations (e.g., Hawaiian kahala) can introduce hormones and antibiotics into ocean waters [vetalapalma, page ?; AoE-85July30, page ?].
* **Genetic Dilution:** The escape of domesticated fish can lead to interbreeding with wild populations, diluting the genetic base necessary for wild survival [AoE85-July20, page ?; vetalapalma, page ?; AoE-85July30, page ?].
* **Feed Inefficiency:** Carnivorous fish aquaculture can be inefficient; it may require five pounds of wild fish to produce just one pound of farmed fish [vetalapalma, page ?; AoE-85July30, page ?].
* **Chemical Contamination:** The corpus notes a 2001 EU crackdown on the carcinogenic chemical chloramphenicol, which had been used in Chinese and Thai shrimp farms, leading to the exclusion of those imports from European markets [shrimp 3EB, page ?; shrimp 2WY, page ?; Shrimp outline2, page ?].
