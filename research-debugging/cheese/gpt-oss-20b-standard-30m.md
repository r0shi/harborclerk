### 1.  Introduction  
The corpus contains a rich set of ethnographic, technical, and historical accounts of cheese‑making in a variety of European and Mediterranean settings. ### 8.  Contradictions & Gaps  

* The corpus does not provide direct accounts of cheese‑making in Spain, Portugal, East Asia, Africa, the Middle East, or South America; therefore comparative statements for those regions are not possible.  
* The manual’s sparse instructions and translation inconsistencies mean that some technical details (e.g., exact temperatures, culture strains) remain uncertain.  

---

### 9.  Conclusion  

Across the corpus, cheese‑making traditions share a core reliance on local flora, animal milk, and microbial cultures, yet they diverge markedly in production methods, ingredient handling, and aging practices.  Greek touloumotiri emphasizes skin‑terroir and shepherd mobility; Swiss gruyère showcases mechanical cutting, heating, and stone pressing; alpine cave cheeses (French, Swiss, Italian) rely on cave‑borne molds.  Modern industrial techniques introduce purified rennet and isolated cultures, contrasting with traditional rennet that supplies both coagulation and culture.  The documented manuals, while valuable, reflect the challenges of transmitting detailed artisanal knowledge across cultures and languages.  Overall, the corpus illustrates that cheese‑making is a culturally embedded craft, with each tradition adapting to its environment, resources, and historical trajectory.
