## **2. Role of Environment and Local Knowledge**

The cheese-making process in some regions, such as the **Italian Alps and the Greek Peloponnese**, involves a deep understanding of the local environment and careful attention to the conditions under which the cheese is made and aged [tsakoniacheese 10, page ?]. This reflects the concept of **terroir**, which includes factors such as location, season, weather, and the choice of method, all of which influence the flavor and characteristics of the cheese [terroir-6-dfs, page ?].

In traditional Greek cheesemaking, **shepherds have historically been the primary cheesemakers**, migrating with their flocks between different grazing areas. They use the **cleaned and salted skins of sheep or goats** to store and ripen the cheese [tsakoniacheese 9, page ?]. The cheese produced in this method is called **touloumotiri**, derived from the modern vernacular "touloumi" [AoE-89-cheese, page ?]. The flavor and texture of touloumotiri vary widely depending on the animals’ forage, with some cheeses having a light grassy flavor and others tasting subtly of wild herbs like oregano and thyme [tsakoniacheese 5, page ?].

## **3. Cultural and Social Practices**

This practice reflects a long-standing tradition that emphasizes sustainability and the transmission of knowledge across generations.

In some regions, **cheesemakers rely on their sense of touch rather than thermometers** to test the temperature of the milk, indicating a reliance on traditional sensory skills [tsakoniacheese 5, page ?]. Similarly, some cheesemakers **pasteurize their milk**, while others do not, believing it may cook out the flavor of the cheese [tsakoniacheese 5, page ?].
