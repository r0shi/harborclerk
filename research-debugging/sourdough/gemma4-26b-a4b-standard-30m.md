### Definition and Biological Composition
*Levain* (leaven) is distinguished from commercial bread yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) by being a spontaneous fermentation of flour and water driven by naturally occurring microflora [painaulevain5WY, page ?]. Biologically, a sourdough culture is characterized as a tandem of a heterofermentative culture and wild yeast [painaulevai3, page ?]. 

Within this environment, specific microorganisms play specialized roles:
* ***L. sanfranciscensis*** metabolizes maltose [painaulevai3, page ?].
* ***S. exiguus*** metabolizes other sugars and is notably tolerant of high acidity and resistant to actidione (an antibiotic substance produced by lactobacillus) [painaulevai3, page ?].

The acidity generated during fermentation is functional, serving to firm the gluten in the dough [painaulevain10, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?].

### Regional Traditions and Evolution
The corpus highlights distinct regional identities and historical origins for sourdough:

* **San Francisco:** This style is characterized by high acidity, with a pH between 3.9 and 4.0 [levain11-25, page ?; No 83-text, page ?; painaulevai3WY, page ?]. Its origin is attributed to Isadore Boudin, a professional baker from Burgundy who opened his shop in 1849 [painaulevain5WY, page ?].
* **Paris:** The Poilâne family produces a *pain au levain* described as dense and chewy, featuring a darkish crumb with sweet wheatiness and subtle, flowery fermentation flavors with a hint of acetic aftertaste [levain11-20, page ?].
* **Le Landreau (near Nantes):** The baking cooperative Pain Virgule, formed in 1992, maintains tradition by using organic stone-ground flour [painaulevain9WY, page ?].

### Regulatory and Industrial Evolution
The tradition of *pain au levain* has been shaped by modern legal standards and industrial adaptations:

* **French Regulations:** Under the 1993 Décret, *pain au levain* must have a pH of 4.3 or below and contain at least 900 parts per million of naturally occurring acetic acid [painaulevai3WY, page ?]. 
* **Controversies in "Tradition":** French law permits the use of powdered levain cultures (composed of selected yeast and bacteria strains) even within the "tradition" category, a practice some purists consider disingenuous [levain11-25, page ?; No 83-text, page ?; painaulevai3WY, page ?; painaulevai3, page ?]. Additionally, 0.2 percent baker's yeast is permitted in the final dough under certain regulations to ensure sufficient oven spring [levain11-25, page ?; No 83-text, page ?].
* **Industrial Response:** To comply with new acidity laws, the manufacturer Lesaffre developed new versions of powdered cultures [painaulevai3, page ?]. In terms of technology, baker Eric Kayser invented a machine for liquid levain that allows for automatic stirring and programmable holding temperatures [painaulevainrecipeAoE1, page ?].

### Traditional Baking Techniques and Equipment
Traditional methods focus on specific ratios, temperatures, and manual tools:

* **Fermentation and Ratios:** While most recipes use a levain proportion of 12% to 20%, some traditional methods use a much higher proportion—up to one-third of the total dough [levain11-20, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr_cuts, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?]. This higher proportion can reduce bulk fermentation time to approximately one hour, compared to the two or three hours required for most sourdough [levain11-20, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr_cuts, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?].
* **Culture Activity:** A fully active culture is identified by its ability to increase its original volume by 3.5 to 4 times within 6 to 7 hours [painaulevain8JM, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr_cuts, page ?; painaulevain9, page ?; painaulevain10, page ?; levainrecipe11-6, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr, page ?; painaulevain9WY, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?; painaulevain8, page ?].
* **Equipment and Workflow:** Traditionalists utilize an old-fashioned oblique mixer with a rotating bowl and a wishbone-shaped arm, as well as a square wooden tub called a *pâtière* to hold the dough [levain11-20, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr, page ?; painaulevain10_wsr_cuts, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?]. Shaped dough is typically placed in linen-lined baskets known as *bannetons* [painaulevain10, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?]. In a traditional workflow, bakers may weigh 2.2-kilogram pieces of dough on an old-fashioned balance to bake into 1.9-kilo *miches* [painaulevain10, page ?; painaulevain11, page ?].
* **Flavor Control:** Flavor and texture are influenced by maintaining specific feeding schedules and temperatures; for example, darker flours, cooler temperatures, and less frequent feeding can produce denser, more acetic loaves [painaulevain4, page ?].
