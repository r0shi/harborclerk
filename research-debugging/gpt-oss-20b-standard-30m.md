### 8. Contradictions / Incomplete Evidence  

* **Oak Usage** – The corpus provides oak details for Champagne, Chablis, and large oak casks but not for Valpolicella, Rioja, Madeira, or German whites.  
* **Vine Age** – Only Bordeaux and Champagne mention vine age; other regions lack explicit age data.  
* **Sugar/Acidity** – The corpus contrasts California adding acidity with European adding sugar, but does not specify sugar levels for other regions.  

---

### 9. Conclusion  

The corpus presents a rich tapestry of winemaking traditions across major wine regions. French traditions emphasize old vines, meticulous blending, and a balance between consistency and terroir (Bordeaux, Champagne, Loire, Chablis, Montlouis). Italian tradition is exemplified by Valpolicella’s distinct white wine. Spanish Rioja focuses on minimal intervention and high‑elevation terroir. Portuguese Madeira highlights fortification and body. German whites are noted for their recent price premium. American traditions showcase climatic influence on tannin and anthocyanin levels, and the use of large oak casks to express soil characteristics.  

These findings collectively illustrate how regional climate, vine age, oak usage, and market dynamics shape the character and production techniques of wines worldwide.
