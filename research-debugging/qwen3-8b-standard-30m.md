## **2. Winemaking Techniques and Traditions**

Winemaking choices, such as the use of oak, filtration, and sulfur additions, significantly influence a wine’s character and taste [terroir-draft-1, page ?]. These techniques vary across regions, reflecting both local traditions and the influence of climate.

- **Oak Use**: The use of large oak casks (foudres) in winemaking can influence the mouthfeel and expression of the wine, offering a more elegant and soil-driven profile compared to smaller barrels [big-oak-casks, page ?].

- **Wild Yeast Fermentation**: In the Muscadet region, small-production wineries often use wild yeast fermentations for scientific, cultural, and qualitative reasons [muscadet_3, page ?]. This practice is associated with a stronger connection between the winemaker and the land, as it limits the diversity and population of wild yeasts in vineyards [muscadet_3, page ?].

- **Rebula (Slovenia)**: The Rebula wine from Slovenia, made using traditional amphora methods, differs significantly from wines in neighboring regions like Collio, highlighting the influence of both terroir and winemaking tradition [rebula 5, page ?].

- **Chenin Blanc (Loire Valley)**: Montlouis-sur-Loire produces Chenin Blanc in various styles, including sparkling and sweet. - **Napa Valley (California)**: Winemakers in hot climates argue that temperature swings, particularly cool nights following hot days, help preserve acidity in grapes, especially malic acid, despite the warm days [terroir15, page ?].
