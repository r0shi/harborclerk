### **Europe**

#### **France**
* **Bordeaux (Médoc):** In the Médoc, some producers (such as Jaugaret) create small-quantity wines using very old vines, including 50-year-old Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot and 100-year-old Malbec [jaugaret1, page ?]. These wines are distinguished from standardized Bordeaux styles by their intense dark fruit bouquets, vivid acidity, and the use of older barrels for aging [jaugaret1, page ?].
* **Chablis (Burgundy):** Chablis is one of France's coolest wine-growing areas and is planted entirely with Chardonnay [chablis2_wsr, page ?]. The region features seven Grand Cru vineyards (Blanchot, Bougros, Les Grenouilles, Les Preuses, Vaudésir, Valmur, and Les Clos) located on the far side of the Serein River, characterized by brown soil "milky with broken stone" [chablis1, page ?; chablis2_wsr, page ?]. There are also 40 Premier Crus and the lesser appellation of Petit Chablis [chablis1, page ?].
* **Muscadet (Sèvre et Maine):** This region is noted for producing high-quality wines that are unique expressions of terroir and can age well [muscadet-2WY-1-1, page ?]. There is a tradition of producing complex dry wines that may take ten years to mature [muscadet_2WY, page ?]. To improve economic survival, top growers are working toward a Grand Cru classification based on "Crus Communals" such as Gorgeois (gabbro), Clisson (granite), Le Pallet (orthogneiss and gabbro), and Goulaine (mica-schists and gneiss) [AoE85-July20, page ?; muscadet_2WY, page ?].

#### **Italy**
* **Emilia (Lambrusco):** Authentic Lambrusco is a traditional, dry, lively, and floral sparkling red wine with a DOC designation [sottobosco 2, page ?]. In this region, it is considered "practically a way of life" [sottobosco 5WY, page ?]. The reputation of authentic Lambrusco has been negatively impacted by industrial "imposters" that produce cloying, soda-pop styled wines using the bulk charmat method [sottobosco 5WY, page ?].
* **Etna (Sicily):** The Etna region lacks a continuous style [outis 4WY, page ?]. Wines from this area can exhibit a "gossamer" delicacy that differs from the "lustiness" of other Sicilian wines, with some radical producers using amphorae and minimal technology [outis 4WY, page ?].

#### **Spain**
* **Rioja:** Rioja Alta is a subzone (alongside Rioja Alavesa and Rioja Baja) known for producing the lightest-bodied wines due to its higher elevation and cooler nights [senorio 5, page ?]. Traditional practices involve minimal farming of red grapes (Tempranillo, Garnacha, Graciano) and white grapes (Viura), utilizing plowing and Bordeaux mixture to manage mildew [senorio 5, page ?].

#### **Germany**
* **Mosel:** Winemaking traditions in the Mosel emphasize utilizing steep slate slopes above the river rather than alluvial soils [mosel, page ?]. There is a regional effort to prioritize purchasing grapes from these steep sites and protecting old Riesling vines [mosel, page ?].

#### **Slovenia/Italy Border**
* **Collio/Brda:** The Goriska Brda vineyards in Slovenia straddle the border with Italy's Collio region, leading some Brda wines to carry a Collio DOC designation [rebula-raw-EB, page ?]. Some winemakers in Brda, such as Jean-Michel Morel, produce wines influenced by Bordeaux and Georgian amphora winemaking traditions [rebula-raw-EB, page ?].

#### **Madeira (Portugal)**
* Madeira production is defined by heating the wine to develop molasses and caramel aromas [madeira 13, page ?]. The highest quality Madeiras are heated slowly in neutral oak casks in warm rooms for years, while cheaper versions are heated in temperature-controlled steel vats for 90 days [madeira 13, page ?]. Producers, known as "shippers," utilize warehouses in Funchal that act as microclimates; higher heat in these buildings accelerates evaporation and concentration [madeira 13, page ?].

### **Australia**

* **Yarra Valley:** This 10,000 to 20,000-acre region in Victoria has a history dating back to 1838 [yarra-4-9, page ?]. Vineyards have been expanded using cuttings from prestigious European vineyards like Château Lafite [yarra-4-9, page ?]. The region's diverse geography allows for various grape varieties and styles, and in this region, Shiraz is specifically referred to as Syrah [YarraValley2, page ?; The Yarra Valley5KKedits, page ?]. A modern trend in the valley involves small, experimental producers who often lack traditional family winemaking backgrounds [YarraValley2, page ?].

### **North America**

#### **United States (California)**
* **Syrah:** Syrah production has grown significantly in California, though the grape is often shunned by consumers [art of eating send, page ?]. California Syrah often lacks the "velvety, smoked-meat scented" character characteristic of traditional Northern Rhone Syrahs [art of eating send, page ?].
* **Napa Valley:** In Napa, temperature swings between hot days and cool nights serve as a "safety net" for maintaining grape acidity [terroir16, page ?]. Mesoclimates vary by hill exposure; for example, Chardonnay vines near western hills receive later morning sunlight compared to those near eastern hills [terroir 1 WY, page ?].
* **Chardonnay Comparison:** Napa Valley Chardonnay is described as darker and heavier with tropical fruit aromas (pineapple, grapefruit), which contrasts with the lighter, apple and lemon aromas of Chablis [terroir 8, page ?].
